MultispeciesOccupancy.jl

Occupancy models for several species at once, with species interactions (Rota et al. 2016). Each site's joint presence of the species follows a MultivariateBernoulli distribution: first-order terms give each species' occupancy, second-order terms say whether pairs of species occur together more (positive) or less (negative) often than that, and both can depend on covariates. Each species is detected imperfectly on repeat visits.

Put the data in, get answers out

using MultispeciesOccupancy

data = mesocarnivores()          # bobcat, coyote and red fox at 1,437 camera sites
fit = occupancy(data.detections; sites=data.sites,
                occupancy   = @formula(ψ ~ Dist_5km + HDens_5km),
                interaction = @formula(ψψ ~ 1),
                detection   = @formula(p ~ 1))

coeftable(fit)
conditional_occupancy(fit, "redfox", "coyote" => true)
conditional_occupancy(fit, "redfox", "coyote" => false)

Scale continuous covariates first. interaction = @formula(ψψ ~ 0) removes the interactions; one species gives the single-species model of MacKenzie et al. (2002).

In PracticalBayes

  • marginal_loglik: the likelihood with the latent states summed out, differentiable for NUTS; add it to any model with @addlogprob!, or use rota_model.
  • rota_gibbs: latent states as parameters, with a Gibbs sampler of exact kernels (LatentStates, DetectionPG) and NUTS for the coefficients.
  • occupancy_loglik and detection_loglik: the two complete-data parts, for building other samplers.

The likelihood agrees with unmarked's occuMulti to rounding error on the MesoCarnivores data (tested). Exact sums over the 2^S joint states suit up to about ten species.

API reference

MultispeciesOccupancy.MultispeciesOccupancy — Module
MultispeciesOccupancy

Occupancy models for several species at once, with species interactions (Rota et al. 2016, Methods Ecol. Evol. 7:1164): each site's joint presence of the species follows a multivariate Bernoulli distribution whose first-order terms (occupancy) and second-order terms (co-occurrence) depend on covariates, and each species is detected imperfectly on repeat visits.

Three ways in:

  • occupancy: give the detection histories and formulas, get a fit with coefficient tables and occupancy probabilities.
  • rota_model and marginal_loglik: the model, or its likelihood with the latent states summed out, for PracticalBayes (NUTS, or a model of your own via @addlogprob!).
  • rota_gibbs: a Gibbs sampler from exact kernels (LatentStates, DetectionPG) that can be extended block by block.

MultispeciesOccupancy.DetectionPG — Type
DetectionPG(data::OccupancyData; prior_sd=2)

A Gibbs kernel for the detection coefficients a: given the latent states, each species' detections at the sites where it is present form a logistic regression, drawn exactly by Pólya–Gamma augmentation.

MultispeciesOccupancy.LatentStates — Type
LatentStates(data::OccupancyData)

A Gibbs kernel for the latent presence vectors z (one per site): each site's z is drawn exactly from its full conditional, over all 2^S joint states, given the coefficients and the detections.

source
MultispeciesOccupancy.MultivariateBernoulli — Type
MultivariateBernoulli(f1, f2, pairs)

The distribution of a presence vector z ∈ {0,1}^S with first-order natural parameters f1 (one per species) and second-order (interaction) parameters f2 (one per pair in pairs, a vector of (s, t) tuples):

log P(z) = Σₛ f1[s] zₛ + Σₖ f2[k] z[pairs[k][1]] z[pairs[k][2]] - log normaliser

f2[k] > 0 means the two species occur together more often than their first-order terms alone imply. With no interactions the species are independent with occupancy probabilities logistic.(f1).

MultispeciesOccupancy.OccupancyData — Type
OccupancyData(detections; species, sites, visits=(;),
              occupancy=@formula(ψ ~ 1), interaction=@formula(ψψ ~ 1),
              detection=@formula(p ~ 1))

Detection histories of several species at the same sites, ready to fit.

  • detections: one sites × visits matrix (or table) per species, as a Dict/NamedTuple of species => matrix, or a vector of matrices with species names, or a sites × visits × species array. Entries are 0/1, or missing for a visit that did not happen.

  • sites: a table with one row per site, holding the covariates the occupancy, interaction and detection formulas use.

  • visits: covariates that vary by visit, each a sites × visits matrix, as a NamedTuple or Dict (visits = (effort = E,)); the detection formula can use them alongside site covariates.

  • occupancy: first-order occupancy, one set of coefficients per species.

  • interaction: pairwise co-occurrence (Rota et al. 2016's second-order natural parameters), one set of coefficients per pair of species. @formula(ψψ ~ 0) removes interactions, giving independent single-species models.

  • detection: detection probability, one set of coefficients per species.

  • scale: site covariates to standardise (mean 0, standard deviation 1) before fitting, e.g. scale = [:forest, :elevation]. The default priors assume coefficients of order one, so scale continuous covariates. Predictions at newdata apply the same scaling.

The left-hand sides (ψ, ψψ, p) only label the formulas.

MultispeciesOccupancy.conditional_occupancy — Method
conditional_occupancy(fit, species, other => present; newdata=nothing) -> NamedTuple

The probability that species occupies a site given that other is present (true) or absent (false), averaged over the sites (or newdata), with its posterior mean and 95 % interval. Comparing the two conditions shows the interaction on the probability scale:

conditional_occupancy(fit, "redfox", "coyote" => true)
conditional_occupancy(fit, "redfox", "coyote" => false)

MultispeciesOccupancy.detection_loglik — Method
detection_loglik(data, a, z)

The log-likelihood of the detections given latent states z: Bernoulli detections at the visits to sites where each species is present. Zero likelihood (-Inf) if a species was detected where z says it is absent.

source
MultispeciesOccupancy.marginal_loglik — Method
marginal_loglik(data, b, g, a)

The log-likelihood of the detection data with the latent occupancy states summed out (Rota et al. 2016, eq. 4), for coefficient vectors b (first-order occupancy, p_occ × S, by species), g (interactions, p_int × P, by pair) and a (detection, p_det × S, by species). Differentiable in all three; use it in any PracticalBayes model with @addlogprob!, or through rota_model.

marginal_occupancy(fit; newdata=nothing) -> DataFrame

Each species' occupancy probability averaged over the sites (or the rows of newdata), with its posterior mean and 95 % interval.

MultispeciesOccupancy.mesocarnivores — Method
mesocarnivores() -> (; detections, sites)

Camera-trap detections of bobcat, coyote and red fox at 1,437 sites, each surveyed on three occasions: the data of Rota et al. (2016) as distributed with the R package unmarked (MesoCarnivores).

detections is a NamedTuple of 1437 × 3 matrices (bobcat, coyote, redfox; 1 = detected, missing = no survey). sites holds the site covariates: Dist_5km (proportion of disturbed land within 5 km), HDens_5km (housing density within 5 km), Latitude, Longitude, People_site (people photographed per day) and Trail (camera on a trail).

data = mesocarnivores()
fit = occupancy(data.detections; sites=data.sites,
                occupancy=@formula(ψ ~ Dist_5km + HDens_5km))

MultispeciesOccupancy.occupancy — Method
occupancy(detections; sites, visits=(;), occupancy=@formula(ψ ~ 1),
          interaction=@formula(ψψ ~ 1), detection=@formula(p ~ 1),
          method=:nuts, draws=1000, warmup=1000, chains=4, prior_sd=2,
          rng=Random.default_rng(), progress=false)

Fit a multispecies occupancy model with species interactions (Rota et al. 2016) and return an OccupancyFit. The data and formula arguments are those of OccupancyData, which can also be passed directly. With one species this is the single-species occupancy model of MacKenzie et al. (2002).

  • method = :nuts samples the coefficients by NUTS with the latent states summed out (rota_model); method = :gibbs samples the states too, with exact kernels (rota_gibbs), which keeps every species' latent presence.
  • chains run in parallel when Julia has several threads (julia -t auto).
fit = occupancy(Dict("bobcat" => yb, "coyote" => yc, "redfox" => yr); sites,
                occupancy = @formula(ψ ~ dist_city + housing),
                interaction = @formula(ψψ ~ 1))
conditional_occupancy(fit, "redfox", "coyote" => true)

MultispeciesOccupancy.occupancy_loglik — Method
occupancy_loglik(data, b, g, z)

The complete-data log-likelihood of latent states z (one presence vector per site) under the occupancy part of the model: Σᵢ log P(zᵢ | b, g). With z given, this is all that b and g depend on, so it is the target of their Gibbs block.

source
MultispeciesOccupancy.occupancy_probabilities — Method
occupancy_probabilities(fit; newdata=nothing, conditional=false) -> Matrix

Posterior mean probability that each species occupies each site (sites × species). By default this is the model's occupancy probability at the site's covariates (or at newdata, a table of site covariates). With conditional=true it is the probability given the detections there ("realised" occupancy): 1 where a species was detected.

MultispeciesOccupancy.rota_gibbs — Method
rota_gibbs(data::OccupancyData; prior_sd=2) -> (model, sampler)

The multispecies occupancy model with the latent presence vectors z as parameters, and a PracticalBayes Gibbs sampler built from exact kernels: the states by LatentStates, detection by DetectionPG (Pólya–Gamma), and the occupancy and interaction coefficients by NUTS. Replace or add blocks to extend the model.

model, spl = rota_gibbs(data)
chn = sample(model, spl, 2000; n_adapts=500, discard_initial=500)
source
MultispeciesOccupancy.rota_model — Method
rota_model(data::OccupancyData; prior_sd=2)

The multispecies occupancy model with the latent states summed out, as a PracticalBayes model with parameters b (first-order occupancy), g (interactions) and a (detection), each with independent Normal(0, prior_sd) priors (prior_sd may be a NamedTuple with occupancy, interaction and detection entries). Sample it with NUTS, or use marginal_loglik in a model of your own.

chn = sample(rota_model(data), NUTS(), 1000)

StatsAPI.coeftable — Method
coeftable(fit) -> DataFrame

Posterior mean, standard deviation and 95 % interval of every coefficient, on the logit scale: occupancy (first-order, one row per species and term), interaction (one per species pair and term; positive means the pair occurs together more often than its first-order terms imply) and detection.
